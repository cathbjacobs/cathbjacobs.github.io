Operating playbook

Integrating acquisitions in an agent-first company

A practical cross-functional playbook for integrating people, products, data, models, and autonomous workflows without sacrificing security, accountability, or speed.

Core premise

Every acquisition creates two integration jobs

The traditional job is integrating people, contracts, systems, finances, customers, and intellectual property. The agent-first job is governing every acquired model, tool, connector, data asset, and autonomous workflow as a system with defined identity, authority, evidence of safe performance, accountable humans, and a recovery path.

Practical rule: no acquired capability becomes a production agent simply because it is technically impressive. Before its authority expands, the organization must be able to answer: who owns it, what can it access and do, on whose authority, with what evaluation evidence, and how can it be stopped and recovered?
Applied to the acquisition report

Different deal types need different integration archetypes

These are recommended integration responses, not claims about OpenAI’s undisclosed internal plans.

Interface and hardware: io, Sky

Archetype: preserve product craft while standardizing security, privacy, identity, and release governance. The integration risk is moving too slowly on user experience or too quickly on device and desktop permissions.

Developer and execution: Astral, Ona

Archetype: preserve ecosystem trust and open-source commitments; integrate the control plane first. The release gate should emphasize secure execution, credentials, auditability, and rollback.

Evaluation and safety: Statsig, Neptune, Promptfoo

Archetype: absorb into the shared measurement and governance stack. The integration priority is compatible telemetry, evaluation standards, and independent escalation paths.

Vertical context: Torch and consumer-focused teams

Archetype: isolate and validate before combining. Health and financial context require special treatment for consent, data rights, retention, accuracy, and human review.

Deployment: Tomoro, Northslope

Archetype: scale carefully as a services-enabled delivery arm. Define account ownership, partner boundaries, customer-data controls, and the handoff from custom deployment to repeatable product.

Communications: TBPN

Archetype: preserve credible editorial and operating independence while establishing clear policy, disclosure, and escalation expectations.

Operating model

Integration Management Office + agent control plane

Integration Management Office

Owns the value thesis, Day-1 readiness, workstream coordination, risk and decision log, clean-team protocols, communications, and 30/60/100-day outcomes.

Agent control plane

Inventories every agent, model, prompt, memory, connector, identity, permission, data boundary, evaluation record, human owner, monitoring plan, and rollback path.

Talent & HR

Retain the people who hold the value

Critical roles, founders, maintainers, reporting lines, retention, culture, and workforce communications.

IT, security & data

Contain before connecting

Identity federation, asset inventory, data classification, access boundaries, secure migration, and incident response.

Legal, privacy & IP

Prove the rights chain

Code, models, training data, licenses, privacy obligations, change-of-control terms, and regulatory mapping.

Finance & procurement

Protect value capture

Baselines, cloud commitments, vendor concentration, retention liabilities, integration cost, and synergy milestones.

Product & model governance

Earn broader autonomy

Risk tiers, release gates, evaluations, red teaming, product decisions, safety criteria, and customer impact.

Communications & change

Keep trust intact

Employee and customer continuity, escalation paths, external claims review, and transparent change communication.

Phased process

Four phases, six workstreams, one accountable owner

Phase 0Pre-closeDiligence & design

Design the integration before systems or teams are combined

  • Write a measurable acquisition thesis: talent, product, data rights, developer ecosystem, distribution, or deployment capacity.
  • Select an integration archetype: absorb, preserve with shared controls, subsidiary-like operation, or sunset and migrate.
  • Set executive sponsor, named workstream leads, decision rights, Day-1 criteria, and a 30/60/100-day scorecard.
  • Inventory systems, models, repositories, credentials, cloud accounts, datasets, third-party tools, and agent connectors.
  • Create an agent bill of materials: model, prompts, memory, tools, permissions, data classes, monitoring, human owner, and disablement method.
  • Use legally approved clean teams and information-sharing protocols before closing.
Phase 1Day 1–30Continuity & containment

Keep customers and employees whole while limiting new risk

  • Protect payroll, benefits, invoicing, support, vendor access, service levels, and incident response on Day 1.
  • Give acquired employees an organization chart, decision-maker, communication cadence, and escalation channel.
  • Federate identity first; do not bulk-migrate data or expand tool authority by default.
  • Give every production agent a distinct non-human identity. Ban shared credentials; use least privilege, scoped tools, short-lived permissions, and complete action logs.
  • Default agents to read-only, sandboxed, or simulated operation until they pass evaluation and receive accountable-owner approval.
  • Maintain an emergency disablement and state-recovery route for each agent, connector, and model endpoint.
Phase 2Day 31–100Controlled integration

Validate value before expanding autonomy

  • Use staged releases: sandbox → internal pilot → limited customer rollout → broader release.
  • Test business value, reliability, safety, privacy, security, accessibility, prompt injection, data leakage, tool misuse, and recovery behavior.
  • For payments, production changes, sensitive-data access, external communications, or employment decisions, require deterministic policy checks and human approval until evidence supports a narrower control.
  • Consolidate only data with documented rights, classification, retention, quality, and access policy.
  • Train legacy and acquired teams on agent incidents, permissions, data handling, escalation, and limits of autonomous action.
  • Publish a scorecard: retention, customer continuity, reliability, security incidents, release evidence, and realized value.
Phase 3After Day 100Scale & improve

Turn a transaction into a durable operating capability

  • Revalidate the thesis: invest further, preserve strategic independence, revise the operating model, or retire overlapping capability.
  • Review permission drift, dormant agents, tool connectors, data quality, model performance, incidents, and regulatory change on a fixed cadence.
  • Run tabletop exercises for compromised agents, unsafe action, prompt injection, accidental disclosure, supplier failure, and rollback.
  • Maintain an evidence pack for every material agent: purpose, owners, data and tools, risk tier, approvals, evaluations, incidents, monitoring, and retirement date.
Non-negotiable controls

What an agent-first integration cannot skip

ControlOperational rulePrimary owners
IdentityEvery agent has a unique, non-human identity—never a shared employee credential.IT/security + product
AuthorityPermissions are least-privilege, purpose-bound, time-bounded, and reapproved when scope changes.Security + business owner
Data boundaryAgent memory is a governed data store; classify, isolate, retain, and delete it deliberately.Privacy + data governance
Human accountabilityEvery production agent has business, technical, and escalation owners.Product + functional sponsor
EvaluationAuthority expands only after capability, safety, privacy, reliability, and security evidence is reviewed.Model governance + security
MonitoringRecord material tool calls, access decisions, outputs, exceptions, and user impact.Security operations + product
RollbackEvery release has a disablement route, state-recovery procedure, and customer-communications owner.Engineering + support + communications

Decision gate for expanded autonomy

Do not ask “does it work?” alone. Ask: Is the use case appropriate for the risk tier? Are the data and tool boundaries documented? Has the agent been evaluated and monitored? Is a human accountable for outcomes? Can the organization shut it down safely?
